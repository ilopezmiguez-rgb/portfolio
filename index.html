RATE SHOP ANALYZER

Pricing intelligence for a US last-mile carrier — shipped, in production, +40% weekly volume on strategic accounts

THE PROBLEM

A last-mile carrier receives thousands of rate shops per day — quote requests — and books a fraction as transactions. Revenue leaders can see the aggregate win rate, but not the decision they actually need to make: which lanes am I losing that I could be winning, and what should I quote? Spreadsheets collapse under the segmentation (metro × origin × zone × weight), and by the time a pricing cycle is approved, the market has moved.

KEY PRODUCT DECISIONS
01
Three views, one filter bar. Analyzer (diagnosis), Opportunity Finder (where to look), Price Recommendator (what to do) are separate surfaces but share a global filter state. One mental model, three altitudes — a user who narrows to METRO=BOS in the Analyzer keeps that lens when they switch to recommendations.
02
Impact score beats raw win rate. Ranking segments by win rate alone surfaces tiny, noisy lanes. Impact = (distance from target band) × segment volume. The recommendations that float to the top are the moves that actually shift revenue — not statistical ghosts.
03
$0.05 steps inside a win-rate band. The Recommendator only suggests moves in the smallest price unit the market reacts to, and only when the segment is outside a configurable band (e.g. 60–85%). This prevents overcorrection and keeps the tool usable by humans, not just a black-box optimizer.
OUTCOME
+40% weekly volume on strategic accounts

The revenue team used the Analyzer and Recommendator to reprice by zone × weight segment on our highest-priority clients. Within weeks, won volume on those accounts lifted 40%+ — driven by winning back lanes the team didn't previously know they were losing, at prices the data said the market would accept.

STATUS

Shipped · In production with the revenue team

DIMMA QC

Quality control & equipment compliance for laboratories — taken from prototype to production with Dimma

THE PROBLEM

Labs run on calibrated equipment, and regulators demand proof that every instrument is in spec — with a paper trail to back it. Most labs manage this in spreadsheets and binders: a technician logs a control reading, someone eyeballs whether it's drifting, and recalibration deadlines are caught — or missed — by memory. When an auditor arrives, assembling the evidence is days of manual reconstruction, and a single overdue instrument can invalidate results.

KEY PRODUCT DECISIONS
01
Daily control reports drive the alerts. Instead of calendar reminders, recalibration alerts fire off the actual control data technicians enter each day. The system watches for drift and flags an instrument the moment it trends out of band — not weeks later when a fixed schedule happens to come due.
02
Statistics run in real time. Every control entry recalculates the running statistics live, so the lab reads its quality posture continuously rather than reconstructing it in a monthly review. The number that matters is always current.
03
Audit-ready by construction. The data model captures who entered what, and when, as a byproduct of daily work. The audit trail isn't a separate scramble before an inspection — it already exists, because the product was designed around the audit from day one.
OUTCOME

Dimma QC replaced the spreadsheets-and-paperwork workflow with a single source of truth for equipment status and quality control. Lab teams save significant manual effort, recalibration is caught early instead of after the fact, and audit evidence is ready on demand. The product went from prototype to running in production with real laboratory teams.

STATUS

Shipped · In production with laboratory teams

ROMANA

Shared expense tracker that minimizes settlement friction

THE PROBLEM

Splitting shared costs is universally painful. Existing tools are either too complex for casual use or too primitive for real group dynamics: late joiners, unequal splits, multiple settlements over time. The gap Romana targets is precise — you just paid, your friends are still at the table, and you need to log it in three taps.

KEY PRODUCT DECISIONS
01
Settlements are immutable. A settlement is a fact — not an editable record. Allowing edits would corrupt balance history. The schema enforces this with a no-DELETE policy, because the right product decision and the right data decision are the same decision.
02
Store resolved amounts, not split types. Whether even, percentage, or by item — expense_shares always stores "person X owes $Y." Balance calculation becomes a simple SUM. New split types only need a new form — zero schema changes.
03
Seven extension points from day one. Multi-currency, recurring expenses, receipt photos — none require schema refactors. The architecture of V1 respects the roadmap of V3. Future phases are planned before the first line of code.
STATUS

Design complete · Implementation in progress (Phase 1: Foundation)

CRÉDITO CLARO

Transparent loan simulator built on a single hypothesis

Try the Live Demo →
THE HYPOTHESIS

Most Argentine credit simulators show a monthly payment and nothing else. Users don't understand how much interest they're paying, can't compare scenarios, and don't know what TNA means. The hypothesis: greater cost transparency increases conversion to loan applications.

KEY PRODUCT DECISIONS
01
Offline-first data strategy. INDEC inflation data was pre-fetched and cached statically instead of calling a live API. This eliminates a class of failure modes in demo contexts — a deliberate trade-off of real-time accuracy for reliability. The product works regardless of external dependencies.
02
French amortization as default. Most consumer credit in Argentina uses fixed installments. Matching the mental model users already carry from their real-world loans is a product decision, not just a technical one.
03
Explicit scope exclusion. Real scoring, personalized offers, and live rate feeds were deferred — documented, not forgotten. V1 validates one thing: does transparency itself move the needle on conversion?
WHAT I'D MEASURE
completion_rate % reaching full results after adjusting ≥1 parameter
comparator_usage signals active decision-making, not passive browsing
cta_click_rate the conversion event the transparency hypothesis is built around
